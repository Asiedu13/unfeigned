## The title

When I became CTO at TTYM Global, I thought it would feel like an arrival. It didn't. It felt like being handed a map to a city I'd never visited and told to give tours.

The honest version: I was the most senior engineer who was still around and willing to own it. That's how most of these things happen, especially at startups. The title is real; the mystique isn't.

## What nobody tells you

The biggest shift wasn't technical. I expected more architecture decisions, more system design, more code review. What actually happened was: more meetings, more ambiguity, more context-switching between the business and the codebase.

Nobody tells you that the hard part of being a CTO at a small company is that you're still an engineer — but now you're also the one who has to figure out why the MoMo integration broke at 11pm, and also the one who has to explain to the CEO why we can't ship the feature they promised the investor.

## The work

What I've settled into: I'm a translator. I translate business urgency into engineering decisions. I translate technical debt into business risk. I keep the two sides of the company speaking the same language, loosely.

The code is still there. I write less of it, but I care more about what it means — which systems are fragile, which are quietly solid, where we're building on sand.

At 22, the job is mostly about learning how to be wrong in public and keep moving. That part nobody warned me about either.
